Day 1

Monday 02/02/2026
By Niels Clarysse

The first day started early, at 7:15 AM, we all gathered at Brussels Airport. Our plane took off at 10:15 AM, and after a short flight, we arrived at Dublin Airport. We swiftly collected our baggage and took a bus to the hotel.

Once we arrived, we had a brief moment to make ourselves comfortable in our rooms and grab some lunch.

After enjoying a good meal, we set off on a city tour through Dublin. The tour showcased several of the city's famous landmarks and its rich history. It was very interesting, to say the least.

After the tour, we had the evening to ourselves. We used this time for some night sightseeing. The city was beautifully lit up because, from January 30th to February 2nd, Dublin celebrates women and as a result, St. Brigid was projected onto many of the buildings. To top off the night, we headed to a pub for our first taste of Guinness.

Day 2 - Afternoon

Tuesday 03/02/2026
By Maurice De Kegel

It started with a short animated video about the origins of distillation, going all the way back to ancient Mesopotamia. The guides kept jumping in with their own commentary and the whole thing moved quickly. By the end of it you understood how this one ancient process slowly spread across the world and became the massive industry it is today. We also learned that the word "whiskey" itself comes from the Gaelic "uisce beatha," meaning "water of life," which I thought was a nice touch.

Then the tone shifted. We walked into a room decorated like a hidden speakeasy, dim lighting and all, and that's where things got a bit darker and honestly more interesting. This section was about Poitín, illegal home-brewing in Ireland, and the constant back-and-forth between bootleggers and police trying to track down their copper stills. Because nobody was regulating any of it, the stuff being produced was often genuinely dangerous. Because of the unprecise distillation methanol would build up in the brews and could end up leaving someone in a coma for days, and more than once people were mistakenly buried alive because of it. It is believed that because of this the stories of zombies came alive. So to solve this problem they tied a string to the wrist of the buried connected to a bell above ground. This is apparently where the whole “saved by the bell” expression comes from. And in some of these underground establishments they'd use an actual coffin as the bar counter. When it started to smell, they knew the person was actually gone. Grim, but the guides told it in a way that had everyone laughing.

After that we moved into a room where the walls were covered in animated portraits, moving and talking paintings of figures like John Jameson and James Power. Instead of just reading about these people on a plaque, you got to watch them bicker and boast about their rivalries. It was a bit theatrical but genuinely fun, and it gave you a real sense of the personalities behind the brands.

The guides used this section to walk us through the differences between Irish whiskey and Scotch. Irish whiskey gets distilled three times and the barley is dried in sealed kilns, which keeps the smoke out and gives it that clean, smooth finish. Scotch usually only goes through two distillations, and the barley is dried over open peat fires, hence the smokiness. Even the spelling is different; the Irish keep the "e" in whiskey while the Scots leave it out.

The tour wrapped up in a tasting room that looked like an old-fashioned bar, all warm wood and low lighting. We tried four different Irish whiskeys and the guides went through each one, covering the flavor, the aging, and what made it distinct. Nobody was rushing us out, so there was time to hang around, chat, grab some photos, and have a look around the gift shop before heading back out into the city. If you're ever in Dublin with a free afternoon, it's well worth the detour.

Day 3

Wednesday 04/02/2026
By Matheus Costa

The alarm went off at 6:45. A hearty Irish breakfast was waiting for us downstairs, so there wasn’t a second to spare: I got dressed and headed straight for the lobby.

Unlike the other days, two company visits were scheduled for our second day: IBM and Kyndryl. We left the lobby around 7:45 and, under the typical Irish February rain, headed to a bus stop. From there, a roughly 30-minute bus ride took us to Damastown, located on the outskirts of Dublin, where IBM has one of its many so-called “campuses.”

This campus specifically was a computer factory that later was converted into an office. Our host, Mr. Peter Bottelier, who had been working at IBM for more than 20 years, shared with us a bit of his and IBM’s history, along with the company’s business vision. I wasn’t expecting to hear that IBM was founded in 1911! According to our host, its longevity and relevance are explained by building long-term solutions instead of catching up with trends, because they tend to become less relevant as the hype towards them fades out.

Four guest speakers from different divisions of IBM were invited to share some words with our group as well. They all had different backgrounds in the company, ranging from a senior support engineer to a junior software developer. The gist of all conversations was that IBM supports both kinds of employees: those who prefer to stick to a career path to gain deep expertise in an area and those who are motivated by the challenges that arise from tackling new areas of expertise.

After eating lunch at IBM’s cafeteria, we took the bus back to the centre, where we had a 2-hour break before meeting the whole group for the second visit of the day. Despite the unwelcoming weather, my friends and I decided to eat ice cream and walk around River Liffey to, then, meet our peers at the Tara Street metro station. From there, we took a 15-minute ride to the closest station to our final destination. Then we walked for about another 15 minutes through a neighbourhood home to many consulates and embassies. Why? Our next visit was to the Belgian embassy.

We were treated to afternoon tea while waiting for our hosts to begin the presentation. Although the visit took place at a diplomatic venue, our goal was to learn about Kyndryl, a technology company that, compared to the other ones we visited during our trip, has a relatively recent history. Originally part of IBM, Kyndryl became a separated entity in 2021, with the goal of offering a wide array of services related to IT infrastructure systems, focussing on innovation.

By the time our visit ended, it was already dark outside. So, after returning to the centre, my friends and I couldn’t think of a better way to treat ourselves after such a busy day: a stop by at a cozy pizza parlour followed by a pub crawling through the bustling night streets.

Day 4

Thursday 05/02/2026
By Alexander Engelrelst

On Thursday, we woke up at 6:30 AM for the visit I was personally most excited about. After another great Full Irish breakfast, we gathered at 8:10 AM ready to brave the Irish weather while going to the Google Headquarters. With the wind tearing through the streets, it really was a bad day to be a trashcan.

After a short wait, we entered the building and each received a visitor badge. We received a tour of the campus, and I must admit that the office was extraordinary. The office featured not only regular workspaces but also a vast array of options to relax, such as game rooms, flight simulators, and even an indoor cinema. There aren’t exactly many offices that can say they offer that much. I loved how the sheer number of coffee corners immediately gave away that it was a tech company’s office. As everyone knows we tech people love our coffee.

We heard from both security experts and software reliability engineers. It was probably the most technical visit thus far which is one of the main reasons I enjoyed it. After leaving the Google Headquarters we went for a very healthy lunch, that being KFC.

At 3:35 PM we gathered in front of the Guinness Storehouse. As someone who likes a stout occasionally, it was quite interesting to learn what it is exactly and how it gets made. The best part was, of course, the tasting. Since it was the last day, a few students had the idea to go have a drink with a larger group. We went to Dublin’s Wetherspoons where we had dinner and a few drinks, which turned into quite an adventure.
