THE HOWEST GOES DUBLIN BLOG

By Group 19-1

We are a high-energy group of Howest students who descended on Dublin, Ireland's vibrant capital, ready to make the most of every single moment. Over a few wild, unforgettable days, we threw ourselves headfirst into everything the city had to offer, and Dublin did not disappoint.

By day, we pulled back the curtain on some of the world's most iconic tech companies. Dublin has quietly cemented itself as the Silicon Valley of Europe, and we were lucky enough to get up close and personal with the innovation happening inside its world-class tech hubs. Walking through those offices and hearing from industry insiders gave us a whole new perspective on where our studies could take us.

But Dublin is so much more than just a tech city, and we made sure to soak that up too. Between visits, we wandered through centuries of history, from the cobblestone streets of Temple Bar buzzing with life and music, to the grand Georgian squares that tell the story of a city that has reinvented itself time and time again. We sampled the food, embraced the legendary Irish hospitality, and found ourselves more than once in a cozy pub with a pint in hand, listening to live traditional music filling the whole room with energy.

By the time we packed our bags and headed home, Dublin had left its mark on all of us, not just as a travel destination, but as a city that genuinely sparked something new. New ideas, new friendships, new ambitions. That is what a trip like this is really about.

Day 1

Monday 02/02/2026
By Niels Clarysse

The first day started early, at 7:15 AM, we all gathered at Brussels Airport. Our plane took off at 10:15 AM, and after a short flight, we arrived at Dublin Airport. We swiftly collected our baggage and took a bus to the hotel.

Once we arrived, we had a brief moment to make ourselves comfortable in our rooms and grab some lunch.

After enjoying a good meal, we set off on a city tour through Dublin. The tour showcased several of the city's famous landmarks and its rich history. It was very interesting, to say the least.

After the tour, we had the evening to ourselves. We used this time for some night sightseeing. The city was beautifully lit up because, from January 30th to February 2nd, Dublin celebrates women and as a result, St. Brigid was projected onto many of the buildings. To top off the night, we headed to a pub for our first taste of Guinness.

Day 2 - Afternoon

Tuesday 03/02/2026
By Maurice De Kegel

When traveling on a study trip, some of the best moments happen when exploring a new city firsthand. Instead of sticking with our lecturers for the afternoon, a few friends and I decided to split off and explore Dublin on our own terms. Our wandering brought us right next to the historic Trinity College, where we decided to step inside the Irish Whiskey Museum. What followed was a journey through time, led by two incredibly charismatic and knowledgeable tour guides who brought the history of the spirit to life.

The experience kicked off with a cinematic overview. The tour began with a short animated video that traced the global roots of distillation all the way back to ancient Mesopotamia. The animation, paired with the guides' energetic narration, set the stage by explaining how an ancient process eventually traveled across the world to become a massive global industry — noting that the very word "whiskey" comes from the Gaelic phrase uisce beatha, which translates to "water of life."

Moving deeper into the museum, the atmosphere shifted as we stepped into a room beautifully decorated like an old-school, hidden speakeasy. This portion of the tour focused on the era of illicit home-brewing in Ireland, known as Poitín. The guides detailed the endless cat-and-mouse game between local bootleggers and the police. Because these homemade concoctions were completely unregulated, they were often highly dangerous — high levels of methanol would occasionally put unsuspecting drinkers into a deep, comatose state. To avoid burying the living, locals relied on a string tied to a person's wrist connected to a bell above ground, establishing the expression "saved by the bell."

The journey through time continued as we transitioned into a newer style of bar, where the walls quite literally came to life with interactive, talking portraits of industry giants like John Jameson and James Power. The guides also broke down the differences between Irish whiskey and Scotch — Irish whiskey is distilled three times for an exceptionally smooth finish, while Scotch is distilled twice and dried over open peat fires, giving it a distinctly smoky flavor.

The tour reached its grand finale in a vibrant vintage tasting room. The experience concluded with a guided tasting of four distinct varieties of Irish whiskey. The guides walked us through the unique flavor notes, aging processes, and characteristics of each glass. After the presentation, there was plenty of time to take photos, chat with the welcoming staff, and browse the gift shop before heading back out into the city.

Day 3

Wednesday 04/02/2026
By Matheus Costa

The alarm went off at 6:45. A hearty Irish breakfast was waiting for us downstairs, so there wasn't a second to spare: I got dressed and headed straight for the lobby.

Unlike the other days, two company visits were scheduled for our second day: IBM and Kyndryl. We left the lobby around 7:45 and, under the typical Irish February rain, headed to a bus stop. From there, a roughly 30-minute bus ride took us to Damastown, located on the outskirts of Dublin, where IBM has one of its many so-called "campuses."

This campus specifically was a computer factory that later was converted into an office. Our host, Mr. Peter Bottelier, who had been working at IBM for more than 20 years, shared with us a bit of his and IBM's history, along with the company's business vision. I wasn't expecting to hear that IBM was founded in 1911! According to our host, its longevity and relevance are explained by building long-term solutions instead of catching up with trends, because they tend to become less relevant as the hype towards them fades out.

Four guest speakers from different divisions of IBM were invited to share some words with our group as well. They all had different backgrounds in the company, ranging from a senior support engineer to a junior software developer. The gist of all conversations was that IBM supports both kinds of employees: those who prefer to stick to a career path to gain deep expertise in an area and those who are motivated by the challenges that arise from tackling new areas of expertise.

After eating lunch at IBM's cafeteria, we took the bus back to the centre, were we had a 2-hour break before meeting the whole group for the second visit of the day. Despite the unwelcoming weather, my friends and I decided to eat ice cream and walk around River Liffey to, then, meet our peers at the Tara Street metro station.

From there, we took a 15-minute ride to the closest station to our final destination. Then we walked for about another 15 minutes through a neighbourhood home to many consulates and embassies. Why? Our next visit was to the Belgian embassy.

We were treated to afternoon tea while waiting for our hosts to begin the presentation. Although the visit took place at a diplomatic venue, our goal was to learn about Kyndryl, a technology company that, compared to the other ones we visited during our trip, has a relatively recent history. Originally part of IBM, Kyndryl became a separated entity in 2021, with the goal of offering a wide array of services related to IT infrastructure systems, focussing on innovation.

By the time our visit ended, it was already dark outside. So, after returning to the centre, my friends and I couldn't think of a better way to treat ourselves after such a busy day: a stop by at a cozy pizza parlour followed by a pub crawling through the bustling night streets.

Day 4

Thursday 05/02/2026
By Alexander Engelrelst

On Thursday, we woke up at 6:30 AM for the visit I was personally most excited about. After another great Full Irish breakfast, we gathered at 8:10 AM ready to brave the Irish weather while going to the Google Headquarters. With the wind tearing through the streets, it really was a bad day to be a trashcan.

After a short wait, we entered the building and each received a visitor badge. We received a tour of the campus, and I must admit that the office was extraordinary. The office featured not only regular workspaces but also a vast array of options to relax, such as game rooms, flight simulators, and even an indoor cinema. I loved how the sheer number of coffee corners immediately gave away that it was a tech company's office.

We heard from both security experts and software reliability engineers. It was probably the most technical visit thus far, which is one of the main reasons I enjoyed it. After leaving the Google Headquarters we went for a very healthy lunch — that being KFC.

At 3:35 PM we gathered in front of the Guinness Storehouse. As someone who likes a stout occasionally, it was quite interesting to learn what it is exactly and how it gets made. The best part was, of course, the tasting. Since it was the last day, a few students had the idea to go have a drink with a larger group. We went to Dublin's Wetherspoon's where we had dinner and a few drinks, which turned into quite an adventure.

Day 5

Friday 06/02/2026
By Tristan Joos

We checked out of the hotel at around 10:00 AM and were informed that we had the remainder of the day free, with the requirement to return by 1:45 PM to board the bus to the airfield. Taking advantage of this free time, my friends and I decided to return to the city center. We visited a shopping mall and several nearby parks, making a brief stop at a local coffee shop to warm up.

After completing our walk through the city, we returned to the hotel to catch the bus back to the airport. During the bus journey, our lecturers conducted an engaging technology quiz about our trip, testing our retention of the experiences and knowledge gained throughout the week. Upon arriving at the airport, we checked our bags and had time until 4:30 PM before boarding our flight back to Belgium.
